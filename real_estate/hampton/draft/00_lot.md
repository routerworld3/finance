## Correction #1 — it's a condominium, not a lot split

I assumed earlier this was building-plus-parking. It isn't. "Madison Capital Condo Unit 1 / Unit 2" plus a shared plat reference (Book 00M6, Page 0047) means the former Sears was carved into a **two-unit commercial condominium** — one 1974 building, split into two separately owned halves. The maps confirm it: 0U1 is the west portion of the footprint, 0U2 the east, sharing a wall. 114,538 + 90,936 = 205,474 sf, which is the whole original store.

Parcel 3002532 was never retired. It's the original Sears parcel; Unit 2 was spun off from it (hence Parent PIN 3002532 on the newer record), and the remainder kept the old number as Unit 1.

Note also that the surrounding parking shows as *separate* parcels on the maps — WL3, COMM, PCA1, PCA2. The naming pattern suggests these are common-area units in the same or a related condominium regime rather than the storage owners' fee land, which would explain why Unit 1's land value collapsed from $3,182,600 (FY2022) to $585,500 (FY2023) when the regime was created. Worth confirming against the plat if it matters.

## Correction #2 — "433" and "462" are classification codes

I read those as deed page numbers. They're **property classification codes** — Hampton publishes a Property Classification PDF alongside the transfer report. 462 = Self-Storage on both records now; 433 was Unit 1's class at the time of its December 2023 sale, before conversion. The real deed references are the Doc # column: **230 014962** (Unit 1) and **230 003155** (Unit 2).

So the two 2023 lines you found aren't the same asset changing hands twice. They're **two different halves of the same building at two different stages**: Unit 2 being syndicated *after* conversion, and Unit 1 being sold as a raw shell nine months later.

That also refines what I said about the $12M. It's still a related-party drop into a 1031 vehicle rather than arm's-length price discovery, but the number isn't fictional — $4.5M bought an empty shell in 2021, and $12M reflects a completed, operating 90,936 sf storage facility. The assessor carries it at $8.4M, and DST offerings routinely price above appraised value because the syndication load is baked in.
